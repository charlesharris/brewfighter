# Brew Fighter

## Phase 1 Concept Brief

### Working Title

**Brew Fighter**

### High Concept

Brew Fighter is a collectible brewing RPG that combines the strategic collection and matchup mechanics of creature-battling games with a lightweight but meaningful brewing simulator.

The player does not collect monsters.

The player collects and develops:

- Beer recipes
- Hop varieties
- Grain varieties
- Yeast cultures
- Brewing techniques
- Equipment
- Rare adjuncts
- Patron knowledge

The player's "party" is a set of beers on tap.

Instead of fighting monsters, the player encounters bar patrons, judges, beer nerds, regulars, brewers, tourists, foodies, and other characters with different tastes, tolerances, preferences, and dislikes.

The player wins encounters by selecting and serving beers that suit the patron's palate.

The central fantasy is:

**Become a legendary brewer by learning people's tastes, collecting ingredients and cultures, developing unique beer lineages, and building a brewery capable of satisfying increasingly difficult patrons.**

---

# Core Design Pillars

## 1. Brewing Is Character Building

Recipes are the game's primary equivalent of collectible creatures.

The player creates beers from:

- Grain
- Hops
- Yeast
- Water profile
- Mash process
- Boil additions
- Fermentation choices
- Conditioning choices
- Adjuncts

These decisions produce a beer with specific attributes, traits, strengths, weaknesses, and special abilities.

Players should feel ownership over their beers because they created them rather than merely finding predefined units.

Two players starting with the same base recipe should eventually be able to evolve it into very different beers.

---

## 2. Patrons Are Tactical Encounters

Each patron has a palate represented by hidden or partially visible preferences.

Example:

**Darryl, Patio Dad**

Preferences:
- Crisp
- Clean
- Citrus
- Moderate carbonation

Dislikes:
- Roast
- Sourness
- Heavy body

Bitterness tolerance:
Low

Alcohol tolerance:
Medium

Possible weakness:
Mexican lager-style beer with citrus character

The player may not initially know all of these attributes.

Each beer served reveals additional information.

The goal is not simply to identify a patron's favorite beer style.

The player should instead reason about sensory characteristics such as:

- Sweetness
- Dryness
- Bitterness
- Maltiness
- Roast
- Fruit
- Citrus
- Tropical fruit
- Herbal character
- Spice
- Funk
- Sourness
- Body
- Carbonation
- Alcohol strength
- Hop aroma

This creates a lightweight deduction game.

---

# Core Gameplay Loop

The main progression loop is:

**Explore → Acquire → Brew → Serve → Learn → Improve → Expand**

More explicitly:

1. Visit a location.
2. Meet patrons and discover brewing-related opportunities.
3. Acquire new ingredients, cultures, recipes, or equipment.
4. Return to the brewery.
5. Brew new beers or modify existing recipes.
6. Select a limited tap lineup.
7. Challenge patrons.
8. Learn their preferences through serving.
9. Win encounters by maximizing satisfaction.
10. Unlock rewards.
11. Improve the brewery.
12. Travel to more difficult locations.

---

# Beer as the Player's Party

The player maintains a limited active tap list.

For an early prototype, use:

**6 active beers**

Each beer functions similarly to a party member in a turn-based RPG.

Example:

## House Pale Ale

Style:
American Pale Ale

ABV:
5.3%

Attributes:
- Malt: 2
- Bitterness: 4
- Hop Aroma: 6
- Citrus: 5
- Tropical: 3
- Body: 2
- Sweetness: 2
- Dryness: 5
- Carbonation: 5

Traits:
- Crowd Pleaser
- Dry Hopped

**Fresh Pour**
Basic satisfaction action.

**Dry Hop**
Strong bonus against patrons that value hop aroma.

**Sessionable**
Reduces palate fatigue.

# Patron Encounter System

Each encounter should feel like a short tactical puzzle rather than a traditional combat simulation.

A patron has:

- Satisfaction meter
- Taste preferences
- Taste dislikes
- Alcohol tolerance
- Bitterness tolerance
- Style biases
- Hidden preferences
- Status effects
- Memory of previously served beer

The player has:

- A limited tap lineup
- A limited number of pours
- Partial information
- Knowledge gathered from previous encounters

Example encounter:

**Patron Satisfaction: 0 / 100**

Maximum pours:
3

### Pour 1

Player serves:

West Coast IPA

Result:

+22 Hop Match  
+8 Dryness  
-12 Excessive Bitterness

Total Satisfaction:

18

Patron response:

"Good hops, but that's way too bitter for me."

New information:

**Bitterness tolerance: Low**

### Pour 2

Player serves:

Hazy Pale Ale

Result:

+24 Hop Aroma  
+15 Tropical  
+8 Soft Mouthfeel

Total Satisfaction:

65

New information:

**Likes tropical hop character**

### Pour 3

Player serves:

Nelson Session IPA

Result:

**Perfect Pour**

Satisfaction reaches 100.

Encounter won.

# Status Effects

Status effects allow beer order and sequencing to matter.

Possible examples:

## Refreshed

Light or crisp beers receive a bonus.

## Palate Fatigue

Strong flavors become less effective.

## Buzzed

Some patron preferences become less strict.

## Stuffed

High-body beers receive a penalty.

## Curious

Unusual beer styles receive a bonus.

## Nostalgic

Traditional styles receive a bonus.

## Hop Burn

Highly hopped beers become temporarily less effective.

## Pucker Shock

Sour beers become temporarily less effective.

## Wrecked Palate

Triggered by extremely intense beer.

Delicate beers become significantly less effective afterward.

This means the order in which beers are served can matter as much as the beer selection itself.

# Brewing System

The brewing simulator should contain real brewing concepts but remain approachable.

The goal is not to simulate every technical detail.

Instead, brewing choices should clearly influence the attributes used during patron encounters.

## Mash

Player chooses:

- Grain bill
- Mash temperature
- Possibly mash schedule

Primarily affects:

- Malt character
- Body
- Sweetness
- Fermentability

## Boil

Player chooses:

- Hop varieties
- Hop timing
- Boil additions

Primarily affects:

- Bitterness
- Hop flavor
- Hop aroma
- Spice
- Herbal character
- Citrus
- Tropical character

## Fermentation

Player chooses:

- Yeast
- Fermentation temperature
- Fermentation duration
- Possibly pressure

Primarily affects:

- Attenuation
- Dryness
- Esters
- Phenols
- Fruit character
- Spice
- Funk

## Conditioning

Player may choose:

- Dry hopping
- Fruit
- Wood aging
- Spice additions
- Lagering
- Bottle conditioning
- Mixed fermentation

Primarily affects specialized traits and flavor characteristics.

# Recipe Evolution

Recipes should evolve over time.

A beer may begin as:

**Garage Pale Ale**

The player modifies the recipe repeatedly.

Possible lineage:

Garage Pale Ale

→ Dry-Hopped Garage Pale

→ West Coast IPA

or

Garage Pale Ale

→ Belgian Yeast Variant

→ Belgian Pale Ale

or

Garage Pale Ale

→ Lower Gravity Variant

→ Session Pale Ale

The game should preserve recipe ancestry.

This creates beer lineages similar to creature evolution or breeding systems.

Players should become attached to individual recipe families.

Possible yeast categories:

- Wild culture

## Saaz

# Patron Collection System

Patrons should also function as a collection system.

When a player learns enough about a patron, that patron is added to the:

**Regulars Book**

This acts similarly to a creature encyclopedia.

Possible progression:

Seen  
→ Served  
→ Impressed  
→ Regular  
→ Friend

Example entry:

## Regular #037 — Denise, Airport Business Traveler

Habitat:
- Airport lounges
- Hotel bars

Known preferences:
- Clean
- Dry
- Moderate bitterness

Preferred styles:
- Pilsner
- Kölsch
- Pale Ale

Known weakness:
Noble hops

Special request:
"Something interesting, but not weird."

Completing patron knowledge provides rewards.

Some patrons may eventually visit the player's brewery regularly.

Regular patrons can provide passive benefits.

Examples:

A plumber:
Equipment repairs cost less.

A farmer:
Unlocks unusual grain.

A chef:
Unlocks food pairing mechanics.

A microbiologist:
Unlocks unusual yeast cultures.

A bartender:
Provides information about other patrons.

# Boss Encounters

Boss encounters should exaggerate the core mechanics.

Examples:

## The Beer Judge

The player must satisfy strict style guidelines.

Deviation from the expected style causes large penalties.

## The Untappd Guy

Has tasted thousands of beers.

Common styles have reduced effectiveness.

Rare ingredients and unusual techniques receive bonuses.

## The Dive Bar Regular

Has consumed the same beer for decades.

Strong resistance to unfamiliar styles.

Hidden weakness:
A carefully constructed craft lager.

## The Influencer

Preferences change based on current trends.

## The German Brewmaster

Strong preference for traditional brewing techniques.

Adjunct-heavy beers receive penalties.

## The Person Who Doesn't Like Beer

Late-game encounter.

The player must identify a beer that works around broad dislike of traditional beer flavor.

# Tone

Target tone:

**Approximately 70% genuine beer culture and brewing knowledge, 30% absurd character comedy.**

The brewing should be accurate enough that players accidentally learn real brewing concepts.

The characters and encounters can be much more exaggerated.

Example:

**Kyle — Level 34 Finance Bro**

Weakness:
Dry-hopped Czech pilsner

Resistance:
Anything described as "basic"

Special ability:
Changes opinion after reading the Untappd rating

The comedy should come primarily from recognizable personalities and beer culture rather than random jokes.

---

# Art Direction

Initial visual direction:

- Pixel art or stylized 2D
- Cozy brewery interiors
- Strong character silhouettes
- Expressive patron portraits
- Beer shown with visually distinct color, head, glassware, and carbonation
- Ingredient illustrations should feel collectible
- Interface should evoke an RPG rather than a spreadsheet

Reference tone:

- Pokémon-style readability
- Dave the Diver-style character humor
- Cozy management game atmosphere
- Classic JRPG menus
- Brewery chalkboards and tap handles

Avoid making the game visually resemble a sterile industrial brewing simulator.

The first prototype should test only the central interaction:

**Can brewing decisions create interesting beers, and can those beers create interesting patron-selection puzzles?**

Do not build the full management game yet.

The prototype should include:

- 5 grains
- 8 hops
- 5 yeast strains
- 3 adjuncts

## Brewing Variables

Support:
- Grain bill
- Hop choice
- Hop timing
- Yeast
- Fermentation temperature
- One conditioning option

## Beer Attributes

Approximately:
- Malt
- Sweetness
- Dryness
- Body
- Bitterness
- Hop Aroma
- Citrus
- Tropical
- Herbal
- Spice
- Roast
- Fruit
- Funk
- Sour
- Carbonation
- ABV

## Patrons

Create approximately:

**10 patrons**

Each patron should have:

- 3-5 positive preferences
- 1-3 dislikes
- Bitterness tolerance
- Alcohol tolerance
- 1 hidden preference

**3 pours**

**100**

**6 active taps**

1. Score against the patron's palate.
2. Provide feedback.
3. Reveal one or more preference clues.

# Phase 1 Success Criteria

The prototype succeeds if:

1. Players intentionally create beers for specific patron profiles.

2. Players understand why one beer succeeds and another fails.

3. Players modify existing recipes after learning about patron preferences.

4. Players develop favorite recipes.

5. Serving order creates meaningful decisions.

6. Hidden palate information creates deduction rather than frustration.

7. Players begin describing beers as if they were RPG characters.

Example:

"My saison is great against adventurous patrons, but I need something clean for the regulars."

That behavior is a strong indication that the core system works.

---

# Important Design Constraint

Do not reduce the system to:

**Patron likes IPA → Serve IPA**

Style names should provide clues but not determine the entire encounter.

The important layer is the beer's actual sensory profile.

For example:

Two IPAs may perform very differently because one is:

- Extremely bitter
- Resinous
- Dry
- High alcohol

while another is:

- Low bitterness
- Tropical
- Soft
- Moderately sweet

The player should learn to think about flavor rather than simple style matching.

# Core Identity

The central concept can be summarized as:

**Pokémon-style collection and matchup strategy, except the creatures are beers you personally design and evolve.**

The defining mechanic is not merely collecting predefined beers.

It is:

**Creating a beer, developing it over time, learning what kinds of people it works against, and eventually building a diverse team of signature recipes.**

A successful player should eventually have a tap list that feels distinctly theirs.
